\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Prognostic prediction models combining high-throughput bulk RNA sequencing with standard clinical covariates frequently report performance gains under standard randomized cross-validation. However, multi-center datasets are subject to center-correlated acquisition differences, raising the question of whether reported incremental gains persist when entire clinical acquisition sites are withheld during evaluation.

\textbf{Methods:} In this pre-specified re-evaluation with documented deviations within the TCGA Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC) cohort ($N = \NPatients$, $\NEvents$ overall survival events across $\NSites$ tissue source sites), we compared discrimination under two validation designs: repeated stratified random 5-fold cross-validation (5 repeats, 25 folds) versus repeated stratified group 5-fold cross-validation partitioned by Tissue Source Site (TSS). The baseline model ($M_0$) comprised an unpenalized Cox proportional hazards specification on baseline clinical covariates (age, sex, pathological stage). The incremental model ($M_1$) integrated clinical covariates with elastic-net regularized top-variance RNA-seq features selected strictly on training folds, using a clinical penalty factor of 0.01 chosen post hoc after v1.0 null results. The pre-specified primary endpoint was the paired difference in Harrell's concordance index ($\Delta C = C(M_1) - C(M_0)$).

\textbf{Results:} Under randomized cross-validation, baseline clinical discrimination was $C(M_0) = \CMZeroRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIMZeroLowRandom$--$\CIMZeroHighRandom$), and the multimodal model achieved $C(M_1) = \CMOneRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIMOneLowRandom$--$\CIMOneHighRandom$), yielding an incremental gain of $\Delta C = +\DeltaCRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIDeltaLowRandom$--$\CIDeltaHighRandom$; Nadeau-Bengio corrected 95\% CI: $\CINBLowRandom$--$\CINBHighRandom$). Under site-held-out cross-validation, baseline clinical discrimination was $C(M_0) = \CMZeroSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIMZeroLowSite$--$\CIMZeroHighSite$), and the multimodal model achieved $C(M_1) = \CMOneSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIMOneLowSite$--$\CIMOneHighSite$), yielding an incremental gain of $\Delta C = +\DeltaCSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIDeltaLowSite$--$\CIDeltaHighSite$; Nadeau-Bengio corrected 95\% CI: $\CINBLowSite$--$\CINBHighSite$). The cross-scheme difference was $\Delta\Delta C = \DeltaDeltaC$ (descriptive 95\% resampling CI: $\CIDeltaDeltaLow$ to $\CIDeltaDeltaHigh$). The $\Delta\Delta C$ interval includes both a modest drop and a modest rise, indicating the study cannot distinguish the schemes, and the primary hypothesis was not supported. Negative control diagnostics indicated that center identity alone carried prognostic signal ($C = \SiteOnlyCMean$), whereas an RNA-to-site classifier did not clearly exceed the majority baseline ($\RNASiteAccuracy$ vs.\ $\RNASiteBaseline$, fold SD 0.029), and permuted targets yielded chance discrimination ($C = \ShuffledCMean$). A deliberate leakage demonstration on real KIRC data illustrated artificial optimism under unnested feature selection ($\CanaryLeakedCMean$ vs.\ $\CanaryNestedCMean$).

\textbf{Conclusions:} Bulk transcriptomics yielded modest consistent gains over clinical predictors under both validation schemes ($\Delta C \approx +0.024$ random, $+0.031$ site-held-out). The primary hypothesis was not supported ($\Delta\Delta C = \DeltaDeltaC$), as incremental discrimination did not drop under site-held-out validation; the 95\% confidence interval includes both a modest drop and a modest rise, so the study cannot distinguish the schemes. Absolute discrimination fell under site-held-out splits for both models, which may reflect inter-site event-rate and stage composition heterogeneity across acquisition centers. These findings reflect a single cohort (TCGA-KIRC), one linear model class, TSS as a site proxy, and a clinical penalty factor selected post hoc.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Developing multivariable prognostic models that incorporate molecular profiling alongside standard clinical staging remains a major pursuit in computational oncology. Numerous retrospective studies have claimed that high-dimensional gene expression signatures improve survival risk stratification over established clinical metrics \citep{herrmann2020benchmark, wissel2023survboard}. However, multi-institutional repositories such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) assemble biospecimens collected across diverse medical centers, introducing potential center-correlated variation in specimen handling, sequencing batches, and patient referral patterns \citep{howard2021site}.

When prognostic models are assessed via standard random cross-validation, observations from the same collection site are shared across training and testing partitions. If molecular measurements reflect site-specific signatures, random splitting may permit algorithms to exploit center-correlated features, yielding performance estimates that fail to generalize to newly observed clinical institutions.

To address this methodological question, we pre-specified an analysis in a version-controlled protocol (tag protocol-v1) to investigate whether the incremental prognostic value of bulk RNA-seq over clinical covariates ($\Delta C$) is smaller under site-held-out validation than under randomized validation in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC).

\subsection{Related Work}
Developing prognostic scores from high-throughput molecular data has a long history of replication challenges. Herrmann et al.~\citep{herrmann2020benchmark} [TO VERIFY] conducted a multi-omics survival prediction benchmark, reporting that regularized molecular models often yield limited performance increments over well-curated clinical variables under repeated resampling. Wissel et al.~\citep{wissel2023survboard} [TO VERIFY] established SurvBoard to benchmark multi-omics cancer survival models, highlighting potential risks of preprocessing and feature selection leakage. In multi-center datasets, Howard et al.~\citep{howard2021site} [TO VERIFY] demonstrated institutional acquisition confounding in computational pathology.

Our empirical findings offer a descriptive comparison to these prior reports. Whereas Herrmann et al.\ and SurvBoard describe benchmark settings where molecular additions frequently provide limited incremental utility beyond clinical baselines, our TCGA-KIRC evaluation observes a modest, consistent discrimination gain ($\Delta C \approx +0.024$ random, $+0.031$ site-held-out) when clinical variables are preserved and RNA features are regularized. Furthermore, while Howard et al.\ observed substantial center-associated confounding in digital pathology, our site-held-out evaluation in TCGA-KIRC found that incremental transcriptomic gain persisted across independent acquisition centers ($\Delta\Delta C = \DeltaDeltaC$), even as absolute discrimination decreased.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study Cohort and Data Source}
Primary solid tumor (sample type 01) records for TCGA-KIRC were extracted from UCSC Xena. Eligible patients had histologically confirmed primary renal clear cell carcinoma, documented overall survival time $>0$ days, available baseline clinical predictors (age at diagnosis, sex, and pathologic stage), and matched STAR-derived transcriptomic counts ($\log_2(\text{count}+1)$). Patients with zero or negative follow-up were excluded prior to analysis. Histologic tumor grade was excluded from the baseline covariate set as it was undocumented (100\% missing) in the harmonized clinical release. The final cohort traced to file comprises $N = \NPatients$ patients and $\NEvents$ observed death events across $\NSites$ tissue source sites.

\subsection{Model Specifications}
Two nested models were evaluated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Clinical Baseline Model ($M_0$):} A Cox proportional hazards model utilizing standardized age and one-hot encoded sex and stage. A slight ridge penalty ($\alpha = 10^{-4}$) was applied for numerical stability using \texttt{scikit-survival} \citep{polsterl2020scikit}.
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Incremental Model ($M_1$):} A combined model preserving clinical covariates under light shrinkage (penalty factor $0.01$, selected post hoc) alongside the top 500 highest-variance RNA-seq features regularized via an elastic net penalty ($l_1\text{-ratio} = 0.5$). Feature variance ranking and scaling were fitted strictly on the training partition of each fold to avoid data leakage. The candidate alpha regularization path was computed dynamically from candidate RNA features, and optimal regularization strength $\alpha$ was selected via inner 3-fold cross-validation on the training set.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Validation Schemes}
We examined two 5-fold cross-validation regimes repeated across 5 independent random seeds ($5 \times 5 = 25$ total evaluation folds per scheme):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Random Stratified Cross-Validation:} Folds partitioned randomly while stratifying on overall survival event status.
    \item \textbf{Site-Held-Out Group Cross-Validation:} Folds partitioned using Tissue Source Site (TSS) codes as grouping variables, ensuring that all patients from a given collection site were assigned exclusively to either the training or the test fold. Sites containing $<5$ patients were pooled into an auxiliary group to ensure fold balance. Due to finite combinatorial allocations of the 12 resulting site groups, repeated grouped CV yielded $\NPartitionsGrouped$ distinct test partitions across the 25 folds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Inference and Endpoints}
The primary pre-specified endpoint was the paired difference in Harrell's concordance index, $\Delta C = C(M_1) - C(M_0)$, evaluated on each held-out fold. The primary hypothesis was that $\Delta C$ under site-grouped splitting would be smaller than under random splitting ($\Delta\Delta C = \Delta C_{\text{random}} - \Delta C_{\text{site}} > 0$). Ninety-five percent descriptive confidence intervals for $\Delta C$ were computed via non-parametric clustered bootstrap resampling ($B = 1,000$ iterations) and Nadeau-Bengio corrected repeated-CV variance estimators \citep{nadeau2003inference}. Specifically, the corrected variance of the mean performance difference across $J = 25$ folds was estimated as:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{NB}} = \left( \frac{1}{J} + \frac{n_{\text{test}}}{n_{\text{train}}} \right) S^2
\end{equation}
where $S^2 = \frac{1}{J-1} \sum_{j=1}^J (\Delta C_j - \overline{\Delta C})^2$, with a test-to-training ratio of $\frac{n_{\text{test}}}{n_{\text{train}}} = \frac{1/5}{4/5} = 0.25$ for 5-fold cross-validation and a Student's $t$-distribution with $J - 1 = 24$ degrees of freedom. For $\Delta\Delta C$, descriptive 95\% intervals were generated via a joint resampling procedure that resampled patients with replacement for the random scheme and cluster-resampled acquisition sites with replacement for the grouped scheme. Secondary endpoints included Uno's IPCW-adjusted concordance index evaluated at 3-year and 5-year horizons.

\subsection{Negative Controls and Methodological Diagnostics}
To test for potential artifacts, four control analyses were evaluated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Site-Only Model:} Fitting survival models using only center identity indicators to measure baseline prognostic information inherent to collection site.
    \item \textbf{RNA $\to$ Site Classifier:} A random forest classifier predicting center identity from transcriptomic profiles to assess site-correlated batch effects.
    \item \textbf{Shuffled-Label Control:} Permuting survival targets independently to confirm that models collapse to chance discrimination ($C \approx 0.50$).
    \item \textbf{Deliberate Leakage Demonstration:} Evaluating a paired comparison between unnested univariate feature selection (performed on the full dataset prior to partitioning) and strictly nested within-fold selection on real TCGA-KIRC data, providing an empirical demonstration of artificial performance inflation. (Planted prognostic signal checks were executed separately as synthetic positive controls).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Protocol Deviations and Methodological Refinements}
Following protocol pre-specification (tag protocol-v1), all methodological adjustments were logged with date and justification in the project repository (\texttt{DEVIATIONS.md}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regularization Grid Derivation:} In initial package configurations, setting clinical penalty factors to $10^{-4}$ inflated the internal maximum alpha threshold in \texttt{scikit-survival} by $10^4$, causing all RNA coefficients to be shrunken to zero across all grid points. We resolved this by calculating the penalty path directly from candidate RNA features with a clinical penalty factor of $0.01$. A synthetic positive control experiment confirmed that planted prognostic transcriptomic signal is detected under this specification ($C = 0.813$ vs.\ $0.532$ for clinical alone).
    \item \textbf{Clinical Penalty Factor Sensitivity:} Transparently disclosing that penalty factor $0.01$ was selected post hoc, a sensitivity audit across clinical penalty factors $\in \{0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$ confirmed that incremental gain is positive when clinical covariates are lightly penalized ($\text{pf}=0.001$: $\Delta C = \DeltaCPfZeroZeroOneRandom$ random / $\DeltaCPfZeroZeroOneSite$ site; $\text{pf}=0.01$: $+\DeltaCRandom$ / $+\DeltaCSite$; $\text{pf}=0.1$: $\DeltaCPfPointOneRandom$ / $\DeltaCPfPointOneSite$), but reverses to negative values when clinical covariates are subjected to equal unweighted regularization ($\text{pf}=1.0$: $\DeltaCPfOneRandom$ / $\DeltaCPfOneSite$), demonstrating that incremental transcriptomic gain exists only when clinical predictors are lightly penalized.
    \item \textbf{Partition Structure and Inference in Grouped CV:} Repeated grouped CV produced $\NPartitionsGrouped$ distinct partitions across 5 seeds. Deterministic Leave-One-Group-Out (LOGO) cross-validation and Nadeau-Bengio corrected repeated-CV variance estimators were implemented to provide complementary inference.
    \item \textbf{Deliberate Leakage Demonstration Two-Arm Contrast:} The leakage check was refined into a two-condition contrast comparing unnested versus nested feature selection on real patient data.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Primary Endpoint: Incremental Discrimination}
Table~\ref{tab:primary_results} summarizes discrimination across all 50 evaluation folds. Under randomized cross-validation, the baseline clinical model achieved a mean concordance index of $C(M_0) = \CMZeroRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIMZeroLowRandom$--$\CIMZeroHighRandom$; Nadeau-Bengio corrected 95\% CI: $\CINBLowRandom$--$\CINBHighRandom$), and the multimodal model achieved $C(M_1) = \CMOneRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIMOneLowRandom$--$\CIMOneHighRandom$), yielding an average incremental gain of $\Delta C = +\DeltaCRandom$ (95\% CI: $\CIDeltaLowRandom$--$\CIDeltaHighRandom$).

Under site-held-out cross-validation, baseline clinical discrimination was $C(M_0) = \CMZeroSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIMZeroLowSite$--$\CIMZeroHighSite$; Nadeau-Bengio corrected 95\% CI: $\CINBLowSite$--$\CINBHighSite$), and the multimodal model achieved $C(M_1) = \CMOneSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIMOneLowSite$--$\CIMOneHighSite$), yielding an average incremental gain of $\Delta C = +\DeltaCSite$ (95\% CI: $\CIDeltaLowSite$--$\CIDeltaHighSite$).

The cross-scheme difference was $\Delta\Delta C = \DeltaDeltaC$ (descriptive 95\% resampling CI: $\CIDeltaDeltaLow$ to $\CIDeltaDeltaHigh$). Because this interval includes both a modest drop and a modest rise, the study cannot distinguish the schemes, and the primary hypothesis was not supported. Figure~\ref{fig:primary} illustrates the fold-level distributions of discrimination and paired $\Delta C$. Contrary to the primary hypothesis, incremental discrimination $\Delta C$ did not diminish under site-held-out validation; rather, modest positive incremental discrimination was maintained in both schemes. However, absolute discrimination fell under site-held-out cross-validation for both $M_0$ (from $\CMZeroRandom$ to $\CMZeroSite$) and $M_1$ (from $\CMOneRandom$ to $\CMOneSite$).

\subsection{Secondary Endpoints}
Evaluating Uno's IPCW-adjusted concordance index at landmark horizons confirmed the primary Harrell's $C$ findings. At 3 years, the incremental value of RNA was $+\UnoDeltaThreeYRandom$ under random splits and $+\UnoDeltaThreeYSite$ under site splits. At 5 years, incremental value was $+\UnoDeltaFiveYRandom$ under random splits and $+\UnoDeltaFiveYSite$ under site splits.

\subsection{Negative Controls and Diagnostic Analyses}
Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} and Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics} display the diagnostic evaluations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Site Prognostic Confounding:} The site-only survival model yielded an average concordance index of $C = \SiteOnlyCMean$, demonstrating that tissue source center identity alone carries non-trivial survival information.
    \item \textbf{Transcriptomic Site Prediction:} The random forest classifier predicted center identity from RNA features with an accuracy of $\RNASiteAccuracy$ compared to the majority class baseline of $\RNASiteBaseline$ (fold standard deviation $0.029$), indicating that the classifier did not clearly exceed the baseline.
    \item \textbf{Permutation Control:} Shuffling survival targets resulted in an average concordance index of $C = \ShuffledCMean$, confirming chance-level null discrimination.
    \item \textbf{Deliberate Leakage Demonstration:} When feature selection was performed on the entire cohort prior to cross-validation splitting on real KIRC data, the apparent RNA-only concordance index inflated to $C = \CanaryLeakedCMean$, compared to $C = \CanaryNestedCMean$ under strictly nested selection (single demonstration inflation $\Delta C = +\CanaryInflationDelta$), illustrating how pre-split data leakage produces artificial optimism.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Leave-One-Group-Out (LOGO) Cross-Validation and Site Heterogeneity}
To investigate why absolute discrimination fell under site-held-out validation, we executed deterministic Leave-One-Group-Out (LOGO) cross-validation across the 11 sites with $\ge 5$ patients plus the pooled group of small sites ($\LOGONGroups$ total groups, $\LOGONEvaluable$ evaluable with $>0$ death events). Headline event-weighted discrimination gain was $\Delta C = +\LOGODeltaCEventWeighted$ (patient-weighted $\Delta C = +\LOGODeltaCPatientWeighted$; unweighted mean $\Delta C = +\LOGODeltaCMean$; $C(M_0) = \LOGOCMZeroMean$, $C(M_1) = \LOGOCMOneMean$; Table~\ref{tab:logo_results}).

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:logo_results}, center evaluations with $<5$ observed events exhibit substantial estimation instability (e.g., site B8 with $N=33$ and 1 death produced $C=1.000$, while site B4 with $N=9$ and 0 deaths was unevaluable). Per-site analysis among centers with $\ge 5$ events revealed clinical heterogeneity: site B0 ($n = 106$, 69 deaths, 65.1\% event rate) exhibited $C(M_0) = 0.7060$ and $C(M_1) = 0.7307$ ($\Delta C = +0.0247$), whereas site BP ($n = 142$, 39 deaths, 27.5\% event rate) showed $C(M_0) = 0.7572$ and $C(M_1) = 0.7822$ ($\Delta C = +0.0249$), and site A3 ($n = 49$, 7 deaths, 14.3\% event rate) had $C(M_0) = 0.6292$ and $C(M_1) = 0.6685$ ($\Delta C = +0.0393$). Because site-held-out folds evaluate on individual centers or small sets of centers with heterogeneous baseline event rates and stage distributions, the observed drop in average absolute concordance may reflect this inter-center variability rather than generalized model failure.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analyses}
Varying the number of candidate RNA features yielded consistent conclusions. When selecting the top 100 highest-variance genes, mean $\Delta C$ was $+\DeltaCOneHundredRandom$ under random splits and $+\DeltaCOneHundredSite$ under site splits. With 1,000 candidate genes, $\Delta C$ was $+\DeltaCOneThousandRandom$ and $+\DeltaCOneThousandSite$, respectively. Excluding the 9 smallest collection sites ($<5$ patients, leaving 11 sites and $N = 511$ patients) yielded $\Delta C = +\DeltaCTinyExcludedRandom$ under random splits and $+\DeltaCTinyExcludedSite$ under site splits.

\section{Discussion}
In this pre-specified study with documented deviations, we investigated whether the incremental discrimination of transcriptomic features over standard clinical predictors is attenuated when models are evaluated on unseen clinical centers. Contrary to the initial hypothesis that site-held-out validation would unmask an optimism drop relative to randomized splits ($\Delta\Delta C > 0$), we observed that bulk RNA-seq provided a modest, consistent discrimination gain over standard clinical variables in both validation schemes ($\Delta C = +\DeltaCRandom$ vs.\ $+\DeltaCSite$; $\Delta\Delta C = \DeltaDeltaC$, descriptive 95\% CI: $\CIDeltaDeltaLow$ to $\CIDeltaDeltaHigh$). The hypothesis was therefore not supported; the 95\% interval includes both a modest drop and a modest rise, so the study cannot distinguish the schemes.

Crucially, absolute discrimination was lower under site-held-out splits for both the clinical baseline and multimodal models. Our deterministic Leave-One-Group-Out CV analysis indicates that this phenomenon may reflect substantial institutional heterogeneity across TCGA-KIRC contributing sites: collection centers vary widely in observed death rates (from 0\% to 65.1\%) and pathological stage composition. When an entire high-mortality or low-mortality center is held out, the resulting fold discrimination shifts, reducing overall average absolute concordance relative to randomized cross-validation where center proportions are evenly blended across folds.

Our diagnostic controls provide additional context on the multi-center structure of TCGA-KIRC. Center identity alone exhibited measurable survival association ($C = \SiteOnlyCMean$), consistent with institutional differences in patient demographics or clinical referral patterns. However, the machine learning classifier predicting acquisition site from RNA features did not clearly exceed the majority class baseline ($\RNASiteAccuracy$ vs.\ $\RNASiteBaseline$, fold SD 0.029), suggesting that broad transcriptomic batch effects were not the primary driver of patient risk predictions.

Finally, our deliberate leakage demonstration on real KIRC data illustrates the peril of data leakage in high-dimensional genomic modeling. Unnested pre-filtering across the entire dataset prior to partitioning artificially elevated the apparent C-index from $\CanaryNestedCMean$ to $\CanaryLeakedCMean$, providing an empirical demonstration of how subtle leakage produces artificial optimism.

\section{Limitations}
This investigation has several limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single Primary Cohort:} While pre-specified and executed in TCGA-KIRC, these findings reflect a single renal cell carcinoma cohort and may not generalize to other tumor histologies.
    \item \textbf{TSS as a Site Proxy:} Tissue Source Site (TSS) codes represent tissue-contributing medical centers but may conflate surgical center, processing pathology laboratory, and batch submission timing.
    \item \textbf{Model Class Limitations:} We restricted our primary evaluation to regularized linear Cox proportional hazards formulations; non-linear survival algorithms or multi-modal deep learning architectures were not examined.
    \item \textbf{Post-Hoc Penalty Selection:} The clinical penalty factor of 0.01 was chosen post hoc after observing that RNA coefficients were shrunk to zero across the entire regularization path under the original specification. Sensitivity analyses across four penalty factor settings ($\text{pf} \in \{0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$) revealed that incremental prognostic gain exists only when clinical covariates are lightly penalized ($\text{pf} \le 0.1$), and becomes negative when unweighted regularization is applied ($\text{pf} = 1.0$).
    \item \textbf{Partition Structure in Grouped CV:} Stratified grouped partitioning over 12 site groups resulted in $\NPartitionsGrouped$ distinct test partitions across 25 folds, necessitating complementary deterministic LOGO CV and Nadeau-Bengio corrected variance estimators.
\end{enumerate}
